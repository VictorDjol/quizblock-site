QuizBlock

Privacy policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026

QuizBlock is a Chrome extension that puts a short learning challenge (find countries on a map, or answer a few questions) in front of websites you choose. This policy explains what it stores and where.

The short version

QuizBlock has no server, no account, no analytics and no advertising. It does not collect, sell or share your personal data. Everything it needs is stored in your own browser.

Daily totals are kept for 60 days, then deleted automatically.

Settings sync. When settings sync is on, Chrome copies your settings (not your progress) to the other computers where you're signed in to the same Chrome profile. This goes through Google's Chrome sync service under Google's privacy policy; QuizBlock itself never receives it. You can turn it off in QuizBlock's Settings, under Your data.

What QuizBlock can see

  • The sites on your list. QuizBlock uses Chrome's declarativeNetRequest rules to send visits to those sites to its own gate page, and shows the time left on the QuizBlock toolbar icon while they're unlocked. Chrome applies these rules itself; QuizBlock doesn't read the pages or what you do on them. If you gate only part of a site (say youtube.com/shorts), QuizBlock checks the address of pages on that site as you move around it, to notice when you enter that part. It looks at the address only, never the page's content, and keeps no record of it.
  • Which tabs have a gated site open. To run the clock only while a gated site is open, QuizBlock checks whether any tab is showing one of your gated sites. It doesn't record your browsing history, and it doesn't look at tabs for any other site.
  • The extensions page, during a focus lock (only if you ask). If you turn on "Also keep the extensions page closed" when starting a focus lock, Chrome asks you to let QuizBlock see your tabs. Until that lock ends, QuizBlock closes any tab showing the browser's extensions page. It doesn't record or keep anything about your tabs.
  • The current tab, when you open QuizBlock's toolbar popup, so it can show that site's status or offer to gate it. This is used on the spot and not stored.

QuizBlock asks once for access to all sites so you can add new sites later without a prompt each time. That access is only used for the sites on your list.

What QuizBlock doesn't do

  • It makes no network requests. Maps and the question bank are built into the extension.
  • It doesn't use cookies, trackers, analytics or third-party code that phones home.
  • It doesn't sell, rent or transfer any data, and doesn't use it for advertising, credit-worthiness or anything unrelated to gating your chosen sites.

Your control

  • Back up and restore: Settings → Your data → Export backup downloads your settings and progress as a JSON file; Restore a backup reads one back in. The file never leaves your computer unless you move it.
  • Erase progress: Settings → Your data → Reset progress.
  • Stop syncing: turn off Sync settings across computers. Settings already in Chrome sync can be removed with Chrome's own controls for your synced data (chrome://settings/syncSetup).
  • Remove everything: uninstalling QuizBlock deletes all its local data from that browser. When QuizBlock is removed, the browser opens one static “before you go” page from this website; it runs no scripts and records nothing, and no information about you is sent with it.

This website

This website (this policy, the homepage and the “before you go” page) is a set of static pages hosted on GitHub Pages. It has no scripts, cookies or analytics. GitHub, which runs the hosting, may log visitors’ IP addresses for security, under the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

Children

QuizBlock is a general-audience educational tool. It doesn't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at the same address with a new date, and the change will be noted in the extension's release notes.

Contact

Questions about privacy, or anything else: open an issue on GitHub. Issues are public, so please don’t include personal details.
